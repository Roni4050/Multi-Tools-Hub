Understanding the Mandatory 4-Megapixel Rule

Many novice stock contributors confuse a camera's sensor resolution with an image's pixel count. When stock agencies state that an image must be at least 4MP, they are calculating the product of the width in pixels multiplied by the height in pixels:

Total Megapixels (MP) = (Width in Pixels × Height in Pixels) ÷ 1,000,000

For example, a standard 1920×1080 Full HD image only contains 2,073,600 pixels (approximately 2.07 MP). Submitting this file to Adobe Stock or Shutterstock results in an immediate automated rejection for "insufficient resolution." To pass the 4MP gate, an image in 16:9 ratio must measure at least 2667×1500 pixels (4,000,500 total pixels).

Calculating 2x, 4x, and 8x Upscale Dimensions Safely

When working with AI-generated art (from Midjourney, Stable Diffusion, or DALL-E) or cropped telephoto photographs, source images frequently begin around 1024×1024 or 1536×1024 pixels. To sell these assets commercially, artists utilize AI upscalers.

Before running lengthy batch upscale operations, use the Aspect Ratio & 4MP Calculator to verify your target dimensions. A 2x upscale multiplies both horizontal and vertical axes by two, quadrupling total pixels. A 1024×1024 image (1.05 MP) upscaled 2x becomes 2048×2048 (4.19 MP)—instantly exceeding the stock submission requirement without wasting GPU cycles on unnecessary 4x over-processing.

Pro Tips to Avoid Agency Rejections

  • Never Upscale with Nearest-Neighbor: Basic bilinear or nearest-neighbor scaling creates noticeable edge blurring or pixel staircase artifacts. Always inspect 100% zoom crops for sharpness before submitting.
  • Inspect Clean Solid Backgrounds: Stock inspectors frequently reject upscaled illustrations that introduce compression artifacts or chromatic noise into pure white or dark backgrounds.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does Shutterstock reject files that are 3.99 MP?
Stock agency submission bots use strict numerical operators (Total Pixels >= 4,000,000). A file with 3,999,990 pixels will be automatically rejected. Always aim for at least 4.05 MP to avoid rounding discrepancy rejections.
Does 300 DPI matter for stock submissions?
Most stock agencies evaluate only total pixel dimensions (width × height) rather than embedded DPI metadata. However, setting metadata to 300 DPI prevents customer confusion when buyers import files into print layout software.

What is the maximum file size allowed on Adobe Stock?
Adobe Stock accepts JPEG images up to 45 MB in file size and up to 100 megapixels in total resolution.
